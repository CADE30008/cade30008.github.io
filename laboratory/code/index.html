Laboratory code¶

The laboratory files, in one folder. Put your own .mat recordings in the same folder as these. Several of them call each other, so keep them together.

Download everything you run in the laboratory from MATLAB Drive, or everything for the unit.

Version 2026.25, 2026-10-13. If this differs from the version in the folder's own README, download it again.

File What it does
level_rig.m Find what your rig needs to sit level, and save it
save_recording.m Save a run under a name you choose
plot_recording.m Look at a recording before fitting to it
check_recording.m Say what is wrong with a recording, when a fit refuses
check_scopes.m What each scope logs, and whether it is dropping data
identify_rig.m Fit the rig in front of you, by hand then automatically, and write it down
rig_calibration.m What the arm costs to hold up, from the same recording
save_rig.m Writes this rig's model and calibration beside your recordings
lab1_fit.m Fit every recording you took, and report how much they disagree
lab1_design.m Design against your fit three ways, and check it at the edges of your spread
lab2_3dof.m All three axes as three loops, with the cascade for travel
lab3_statespace.m The same machine by state feedback, with LQR
heli3d_model.m The linearised six-state model, from the published constants
fit_second_order.m The fitter lab1_fit calls
heli_check_one.m The flight envelope
heli_plant.m Reads the fitted model
heli_envelope.m The limits, in one place
elevation_plant.json A fitted model, to compare yours against

Order¶

lab1_fit first, on your own recordings from Part 1. It saves my_model.mat.

Then the single-axis design, which is the same flow as the first session's s2_tune.

lab2_3dof and lab3_statespace are the extension, and they do not need your recordings: they work from the manufacturer's model of the whole machine. Read the comments at the top of each before running them, particularly about what that model does and does not include.

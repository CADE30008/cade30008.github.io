Three minutes. Position and velocity are convenient, not required. Any pair of independent combinations of them describes the same motion. The transformation is applied to all three matrices and not only to A: the states have changed, so the paths into them and the combinations taken out of them change with them. D is untouched, since it bypasses the states. The modal form is where this is used in practice. A diagonal A-hat means the states do not drive each other, so the response is a sum of independent modes and modal analysis is reading them off. Eigenvalues are unchanged by the transformation, so the poles are the same: the system has not moved, only the axes it is described in.

Ten minutes. Expected result: with both motors at V the elevation rows give an elevation acceleration of 2*b_e*V, so G_e(s) = 2*b_e/s^2, a double integrator with no damping and no natural frequency. The laboratory fit is a lightly damped second order, K = 3.4, wn = 1.0, zeta = 0.06. The two are different models. Points for the review: * the manufacturer's model is taken about level flight and omits aerodynamic and friction terms; * the fitted model is taken from a recording about a trim, and its damping is measured; * a model is used where it was identified, and is validated against data to which it was not fitted. For a pair that finishes early: the pitch axis, driven by the difference of the voltages, and travel, driven by neither.

Four minutes. A(6,2) is the coupling between the axes, and the reason the three cannot be treated as three independent problems. Six zeros is not an error. The only entries in A are the three kinematic ones and the pitch-to-travel term, so no state feeds back into its own chain: A is nilpotent, A^4 = 0 exactly, and the characteristic polynomial is s^6. Every axis is a chain of integrators, and travel is four of them in series from a voltage difference. Then the question, which the activity has just set up. What to draw out: * the operating point. This is level flight with the arm balanced, where the gravity moment goes as cos(elevation) and its slope is zero. Their recording was taken about a trim, with the motors loaded; * what is missing. No aerodynamic damping, no bearing friction, no motor lag. Those are what put -0.06 +- 1.00j in their fit; * neither is wrong. A model holds near where it was identified, which is why it is validated against data it was not fitted to.

Three minutes. The definition is strong: any state to any other state, not merely somewhere useful. The matrix is rarely square, so the test is on rank and not on a determinant. Full rank here means full row rank: n independent directions among the nm columns.

Two minutes. The last line prevents the commonest misreading. An uncontrollable system can be perfectly stable; there is simply a combination of states that no input will move. The reason nothing new appears after n-1 terms is Cayley-Hamilton: A^n is a combination of the lower powers. Say it only if asked.

Three minutes, to close the hour. The honest close on the tests: they are necessary conditions and nothing more. Degree of controllability, and how well conditioned the problem is, are what the rest of modern control is for. Then the practical line, which is what most designs actually do. The Michigan tutorial is the example to point at: it designs an LQR controller on all four states of an inverted pendulum, gets a response it likes, and only then says that full-state feedback "is not necessarily valid" and designs an estimator. That is the order the separation principle permits. Worth saying for the coursework: nothing in Part B requires an observer. The reason to know this is that a design which assumes full state feedback has made an assumption, and saying so is part of defending it.
